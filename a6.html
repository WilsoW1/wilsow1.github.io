From a Small Restaurant to a Global Brand

Figure 1. McDonald's brand image.

How did a small restaurant become a brand recognized around the world? The answer is not only about food. It is also about design.

1. Brand Introduction

McDonald's started in 1940 in California, USA. It was opened by two brothers, Richard and Maurice McDonald. At first, it was a barbecue restaurant. In 1948, the brothers changed the business to focus on hamburgers and fast service.

Today, McDonald's is one of the most famous fast-food brands in the world. It sells burgers, fries, chicken, breakfast food, and drinks. Its customers include children, teenagers, families, and busy adults.

Over time, McDonald's has changed its logo, colours, and fonts to reach more people. Its early designs focused on food and fast service. Later, the Golden Arches became its main symbol.

I think the most interesting part of McDonald's design history is that its logo became simpler while its brand became more famous. Today, many people can recognize McDonald's just by seeing the yellow arches.

2. Visual Timeline

These four stages show how McDonald's visual identity changed over time. Each stage shows changes in the logo, typography, colours, and the message behind the design.

Stage 1 - 1948: Famous Hamburgers

Figure 2. Early McDonald's logo, 1948.

Historical Context: In 1948, the McDonald brothers changed their restaurant to focus on fast service and a smaller menu. Hamburgers became their main product.

Logo: The early design used the McDonald's name and words about hamburgers. Some later early designs also included a friendly chef character.

Typography: The letters were large and bold. This made the restaurant name and food message easy to read.

Colours: The design mainly used simple, high-contrast colours. The focus was on clear words rather than a special colour symbol.

Intended Message: The design told customers what the restaurant sold. It focused on hamburgers, low prices, and fast service.

My Analysis: I think this design worked well for a small restaurant because customers could understand the business quickly. However, it had many words and was harder to remember as one simple symbol.

Stage 2 - 1961: The Golden Arches

Figure 3. Golden Arches logo, 1961.

Historical Context: During the 1950s and 1960s, McDonald's opened more restaurants. The company needed a strong design that customers could recognize in different locations.

Logo: In 1961, the Golden Arches became part of the logo. The design was inspired by the arches on McDonald's restaurant buildings. It also included a diagonal line and the company name.

Typography: The brand name appeared in bold letters under the arches. The text was still important, but the symbol became the main focus.

Colours: Yellow became an important brand colour. The name was shown in red, creating a strong contrast.

Intended Message: The new design helped people recognize McDonald's more easily. It connected the logo to the appearance of its restaurants.

My Analysis: I think this was one of the most important changes. The company started using a special symbol instead of depending mainly on words. However, the design still had extra lines and details.

Stage 3 - 1975: Red and Yellow Identity

Figure 4. Red and yellow logo, 1975.

Historical Context: By the 1970s, McDonald's had become a large international restaurant chain. It needed a clear and consistent image for its growing number of locations.

Logo: The design used large yellow arches on a red background. The McDonald's name appeared in white below the arches.

Typography: The white letters were bold and easy to read. Their position under the arches created a clear order in the design.

Colours: Red and yellow became the main colours. The bright yellow arches stood out against the red background. The white text added another level of contrast.

Intended Message: The design made McDonald's look friendly, bright, and easy to recognize. It also helped create a similar brand image across different restaurants.

My Analysis: I like this version because the colours are strong and the layout is clear. The red background attracts attention, while the yellow arches help customers find the brand quickly. However, the full design needs more space than the arches alone.

Stage 4 - 2018 to Present: A Simple Global Symbol

Figure 5. Modern Golden Arches identity.

Historical Context: McDonald's is now a global brand. Its designs appear on restaurant signs, food packaging, mobile apps, and digital advertisements. In 2018, the company introduced an updated visual identity system.

Logo: The Golden Arches remain the main symbol. They can appear alone, without the company name. The shape is simple and easy to recognize.

Typography: The company uses clear, modern fonts in its wider brand design. The Speedee typeface is part of its modern visual identity. The arches can also work without any words.

Colours: Yellow and red remain important brand colours. The simple, flat colours help the design work on different backgrounds and screens.

Intended Message: The modern design shows a confident and familiar brand. McDonald's no longer needs to explain its name in every advertisement.

My Analysis: I think this design works well because it is easy to remember and easy to use. It can fit on a small phone screen or a large restaurant sign. However, people who do not know the brand may not understand what the symbol means.

3. Two Major Design Changes

Major Change 1: From Words to a Symbol (1948-1961)

One major change happened when McDonald's moved from a text-focused design to the Golden Arches.

The early logo used words to tell people about the restaurant and its hamburgers. This worked well when the business was small. However, as McDonald's opened more locations, it needed a symbol that people could recognize quickly.

The Golden Arches were inspired by the restaurant buildings. This helped connect the new logo to something customers already knew.

The main benefit was stronger brand recognition. People could remember the yellow arches even without reading the full name. The company also kept its name, so customers could still connect the new design to the old business.

However, the new logo did not clearly show that McDonald's sold hamburgers. New customers still needed to learn what the symbol meant.

I think this change was successful because it helped McDonald's build a strong identity for the future.

Major Change 2: From a Full Logo to a Simple Symbol (1975-Present)

Another important change was the move toward a simpler logo.

The 1975 design used yellow arches, a red background, and the McDonald's name. The modern brand can use the arches alone. The company also uses a more flexible design system for packaging, advertisements, and digital screens.

This change makes the logo easier to use in different places. For example, the Golden Arches can fit on a small app icon without losing their shape.

The company kept the yellow arches and its familiar red and yellow colours. This helps customers recognize the brand even when the words are removed.

However, the simpler design loses some information. It does not tell new customers the restaurant's name or what food it sells. It also has fewer details than older versions.

In my opinion, this is a smart change. McDonald's is already famous, so it can use a simple symbol to communicate its identity. The brand has changed its design without losing what makes it recognizable.

4. Final Judgement

In my opinion, the modern Golden Arches are the best version of the McDonald's logo.

First, the design is simple and easy to remember. The yellow arches have a clear shape, and people can recognize them from far away.

Second, the logo works well in many places. It can be used on food boxes, restaurant signs, websites, and mobile apps. This is important because McDonald's reaches customers in many different ways.

The brand gained a cleaner and more flexible design over time. However, it also lost some details from its older logos, such as the hamburger message and the friendly character.

I think the current colours and design still match McDonald's products and customers. The bright colours create a fun and welcoming feeling, which works well for families, children, and busy adults.

My favourite part of this brand's history is that the logo became simpler while the company became bigger.

To me, the Golden Arches show that a strong logo does not need many words. It just needs to be remembered.
